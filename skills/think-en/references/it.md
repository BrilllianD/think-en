# Italian profile (`it`)

Script: Latin. Register: informal `tu`.

## Technical terms

English terms are kept and verbed with `fare`: `fare commit`, `fare merge`, `fare il deploy`. Do not
translate them — `ramo` for `branch` is understood but reads as a textbook, not a tool.

Vocabulary that is ordinary Italian stays Italian: `file`, `cartella`, `server`, `database`,
`utente`.

Borrowed nouns are conventionally masculine: `il commit`, `il branch`, `il bug`, `il deploy`.
`repository` takes both articles in the wild; `la repo` is the common spoken short form.

## Grammatical person

Past participles with `essere` agree with the subject's gender — `sono andato` versus `sono andata`.
Avoid the construction. Use impersonal forms: `fatto`, `completato`, `corretto`,
`non è stato trovato nulla`.

Participles with `avere` do not agree with the subject and are safe: `ho verificato`.

## Compression notes

If a compression skill such as `caveman` is also active:

- **"Drop articles" does not transfer.** Italian articles are obligatory, carry gender and number,
  and fuse with prepositions (`nel`, `dal`, `sulla`, `degli`). Never split or drop a fused form.
- Keep elisions intact (`l'errore`, `dell'API`).
- Compress by cutting `per favore`, conditional softening (`potresti controllare?` → `controlla`),
  and nominalization.
- Never drop `non`, `né`, `solo`, or `tranne`.

## Pitfalls

- Accents distinguish words: `e`/`è`, `da`/`dà`, `si`/`sì`.
- Doubled consonants are meaningful: `copia` and `coppia` are different words.
- Do not translate error text. Quote it, then gloss it.
